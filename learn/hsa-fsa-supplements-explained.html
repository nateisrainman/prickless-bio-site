Can you use an HSA or FSA for supplements? An honest guide

The short answer is: sometimes, and it depends on a specific piece of paperwork. Here is how health spending accounts really treat supplements, what a Letter of Medical Necessity changes, and where checkout tools like TrueMed and Sika Health fit in.

Reviewed by the Prickless Bio Team Updated August 2026 6 min read

Health Savings Accounts and Flexible Spending Accounts are one of the few genuinely good deals in personal finance: you set aside money before it is taxed and spend it on qualified medical costs. Naturally, people want to know if their daily supplement counts. The honest answer has a few moving parts, so let us walk through them without hand-waving, and with a clear reminder up front that this is general education, not tax advice.

Key takeaways

  • HSAs and FSAs cover qualified medical expenses defined by IRS Section 213(d).
  • Supplements for general health are generally not eligible on their own.
  • A Letter of Medical Necessity from a provider can make an otherwise ineligible item eligible.
  • Platforms like TrueMed and Sika Health run a telehealth check and issue that letter when you qualify.
  • You, the account holder, are ultimately responsible for IRS compliance, so keep your documentation.

How HSAs and FSAs decide what counts

Both account types are built around the same core idea. Money goes in before income tax, and it can be spent on qualified medical expenses. The definition of a qualified medical expense comes from Section 213(d) of the Internal Revenue Code, and the IRS spells out examples in Publication 502. The guiding principle is that an expense generally qualifies when it is for the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of a specific disease or medical condition, rather than for general well-being.

That last distinction is the whole game for supplements. A product bought simply to be healthier is treated differently than a product a clinician recommends to address a diagnosed condition.

Why most supplements are not eligible by default

Because a typical daily supplement is marketed and used for general health, it usually falls on the not eligible side of that line by default. The IRS has historically treated vitamins and supplements taken for general good health as personal expenses, not medical ones. So if you simply add a supplement to your cart and try to pay with an HSA card, in many cases it will not be treated as an eligible expense.

Our honest read

We are not going to slap an HSA eligible badge on the label and imply it is automatic, because that would be misleading. The truthful version is that eligibility for a supplement usually depends on a provider determining it is appropriate for your specific situation and documenting that. When that condition is met, the pre-tax savings are real. When it is not, they are not.

The piece that changes the answer: a Letter of Medical Necessity

Here is the mechanism that moves a supplement from not eligible to potentially eligible. A Letter of Medical Necessity, or LMN, is documentation from a licensed healthcare provider stating that a specific product is recommended to treat or mitigate a specific medical condition. With a valid LMN on file, an item that would otherwise be a personal expense can qualify for HSA or FSA use. The letter is not a loophole. It is the standard, recognized way the system handles items that are medical for one person and general wellness for another.

Where TrueMed and Sika Health come in

Getting an LMN used to mean a separate doctor visit and a paperwork chase, which is why almost no one bothered. Companies such as TrueMed and Sika Health streamline it. At checkout, you complete a short health survey. A licensed provider reviews it, and if you qualify, the platform issues the Letter of Medical Necessity and lets you pay with your HSA or FSA funds for the eligible purchase. The friction that used to kill the whole idea is largely removed, which is why you now see this option on more wellness sites.

Two honest caveats. First, qualification is individual. Not everyone will qualify, and that determination is made by the provider, not by us. Second, the account holder remains responsible for following IRS rules and keeping records, including the LMN and receipts, in case of an audit.

Frequently asked questions

Are dietary supplements HSA or FSA eligible?
Supplements taken for general health are generally not eligible. However, a supplement can become eligible when a licensed provider recommends it to treat or mitigate a specific medical condition and documents that in a Letter of Medical Necessity. Eligibility depends on your individual situation, your plan, and IRS rules.
What is a Letter of Medical Necessity?
A Letter of Medical Necessity, or LMN, is documentation from a licensed healthcare provider stating that a product is recommended to treat or mitigate a specific medical condition. It is what can move a supplement from not eligible to potentially eligible for HSA or FSA reimbursement.
How do TrueMed and Sika Health work?
They are platforms that run a short telehealth qualification at checkout. If a licensed provider determines you qualify, they can issue a Letter of Medical Necessity so you can use HSA or FSA funds for an eligible purchase. Final eligibility and compliance remain the responsibility of the account holder.
